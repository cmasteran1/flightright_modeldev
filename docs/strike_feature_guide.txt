================================================================================
STRIKE FEATURE GUIDE — Prediction-Time Data Collection & Interpretation
FlightRight v8 Departure/Arrival Delay Models
================================================================================

This document explains the 3 strike/labor-action features, what they mean,
how to obtain the data to compute them for future flights, and recommended
monitoring sources.


================================================================================
1. FEATURE DEFINITIONS
================================================================================

strike_severity  (int8, 0-5)
    The maximum severity of any active labor action affecting this flight on
    the scheduled departure date.

    Scale:
      0 = No active labor action
      1 = Strike authorization vote in progress (no operational impact yet)
      2 = Informational picketing (symbolic, minimal operational impact)
      3 = Partial work action — sickout, work slowdown, or contractor strike
          at specific airports (e.g., ground handler strikes)
      4 = Full airline strike — carrier-wide work stoppage
      5 = System-wide disruption — ATC staffing crisis, government shutdown
          affecting air traffic control, nationwide labor action

    Matching logic:
      - If the flight's airline has an active action, match on airline
      - If the action is system_wide or airline="ALL", match all flights
      - If the action is airport_specific, match flights departing from
        affected airports
      - When multiple actions overlap, take the maximum severity

    Correlation with delay (from analysis on 2.5M WN flights 2024-2025):
      Severity 0: 24.2% delay rate (15+ min), mean delay 14.6 min
      Severity 1: 20.7% delay rate, mean delay 12.1 min (auth votes only)
      Severity 2:  7.2% delay rate, mean delay  3.5 min (single-day picket)
      Severity 5: 29.0% delay rate, mean delay 15.8 min (ATC shutdown)

    Note: Severity 1-2 showed LOWER delays than baseline because authorization
    votes and picketing tend to happen on normal operating days with no actual
    disruption. The model learns to distinguish severity levels — only 3+ has
    real operational impact.


days_to_strike  (float, 0-90 or NaN)
    Days until the nearest announced strike/labor action affecting the flight's
    airline. Captures the pre-strike disruption ramp — airlines begin pre-
    canceling flights, crews call in sick, and operations degrade in the days
    leading up to an announced action.

      Positive = strike is in the future (e.g., 14 = strike starts in 14 days)
      0 = strike day
      NaN = no announced strike within 90-day window for this carrier

    Matching logic:
      - Looks at all events where airline matches (or system_wide/ALL)
      - Computes days from FlightDate to each event's action_start_date
      - Takes the minimum positive value within 90 days

    Correlation: Pearson r=0.104 with DepDelayMinutes.
      Flights 0-7 days from a strike: mean delay 13.0 min
      Flights 60-90 days from a strike: mean delay 21.1 min
      (The higher delay at 60-90 days reflects that the "90-day window" often
       captures flights during other disruption periods. The model learns the
       non-linear relationship.)


carrier_delay_rate_anomaly_7d  (float, typically -2 to +3)
    Z-score of the carrier's 7-day CarrierDelay rate compared to its own
    60-day rolling baseline. This is a continuous anomaly detector that fires
    on ANY unusual spike in carrier-caused delays, including:
      - Undocumented labor actions (sickouts not formally announced)
      - IT system outages
      - Crew scheduling failures
      - Maintenance crises

    Computation:
      1. For each (airline, date), compute the fraction of flights with
         CarrierDelay > 0 over the prior 7 days (shifted by 1 day)
      2. Compute the 60-day rolling mean and std of that same rate
      3. Z-score = (7d_rate - 60d_mean) / max(60d_std, 0.01)

    Values:
       0.0 = carrier delay rate is at its normal baseline
      +1.0 = carrier delay rate is 1 std above normal
      +2.0 = carrier delay rate is 2 std above normal (significant anomaly)
      -1.0 = carrier delay rate is unusually low

    Correlation: Spearman r=0.116 with DepDelayMinutes.
      Q1 (lowest anomaly): mean delay 10.8 min
      Q4 (highest anomaly): mean delay 17.5 min (+6.6 min difference)

    This is the strongest strike-related feature because it fires continuously
    (not just on labeled events) and captures the ACTUAL operational impact
    rather than the declared labor action.

    At prediction time: computed from FlightLabs historical flight data using
    the same rolling window logic as other carrier delay baselines. No
    additional data source needed.


================================================================================
2. HOW TO OBTAIN STRIKE DATA FOR FUTURE FLIGHTS
================================================================================

The strike data is maintained in a curated CSV file:
  ../flightrightdata/data/meta/us_aviation_labor_actions.csv

This file must be updated when new labor actions are announced. Here is where
to find that information and how to monitor it.


--- 2a. PRIMARY MONITORING SOURCES ---

(1) National Mediation Board (NMB)  —  https://www.nmb.gov
    What: The NMB mediates all airline labor disputes under the Railway Labor
          Act. When mediation fails, the NMB "releases" the parties, starting
          a mandatory 30-day cooling-off period. After 30 days, a strike
          becomes legal.
    Lead time: 30+ days before any legal airline strike
    What to watch:
      - NMB case docket: https://www.nmb.gov/representation/
      - Federal Register notices: search "National Mediation Board" at
        https://www.federalregister.gov/agencies/national-mediation-board
      - NMB press releases (infrequent but authoritative)
    How to monitor: Check monthly. NMB releases are rare (a few per decade
    for airlines). The Federal Register publishes formal notices.

(2) Union press releases — the fastest public signal
      ALPA (pilots):           https://www.alpa.org/news-and-events/news-room
      AFA-CWA (flight attend): https://www.afacwa.org/press
      SWAPA (SW pilots):       https://www.swapa.org/news/
      TWU Local 556 (SW FAs):  https://www.twu556.org/
      APFA (AA flight attend): https://www.apfa.org/news/
      APA (AA pilots):         https://www.alliedpilots.org/News
      IBT/Teamsters (ground):  https://teamster.org/divisions/airline/
    What to watch:
      - Strike authorization vote announcements (gives weeks of lead time)
      - Strike date announcements (gives days of lead time)
      - Contract ratification votes (signals resolution — update end_date)
    How to monitor: RSS feeds where available, or check weekly during active
    negotiations. Most unions post authorization votes prominently.

(3) BLS Work Stoppages  —  https://www.bls.gov/wsp/
    What: Bureau of Labor Statistics tracks all major US work stoppages
          involving 1,000+ workers. Published monthly.
    Lead time: Retrospective only (confirms events, doesn't predict them)
    Use: Validate that your CSV captured all significant events.
    How to monitor: Check monthly report for aviation entries.


--- 2b. SECONDARY / NEWS SOURCES ---

(4) Aviation trade press
      - Simple Flying:    https://simpleflying.com (search "strike" or "union")
      - The Points Guy:   https://thepointsguy.com
      - Aviation Week:    https://aviationweek.com
      - View from the Wing: https://viewfromthewing.com
    These outlets cover labor disputes earlier than mainstream media.

(5) News APIs (for automated monitoring, optional)
      - NewsAPI.org (free tier: 100 req/day)
        Query: "airline strike" OR "pilot strike" OR "flight attendant strike"
                OR "air traffic controller" OR "airline labor"
        Endpoint: https://newsapi.org/v2/everything?q=...&language=en
      - GDELT Project (free, real-time global news monitoring)
        https://www.gdeltproject.org/
        Search themes: LABOR_STRIKE, AVIATION
    These can be used to build an alert system that flags potential events
    for manual review. Do NOT auto-ingest — use as a trigger to update the
    curated CSV after human verification.

(6) FAA advisories (for ATC/system-wide events)
      - FAA NOTAM system: https://notams.aim.faa.gov/
      - FAA Air Traffic Activity: https://aspm.faa.gov/
    Relevant for government shutdowns, ATC staffing crises, and system-wide
    ground stops that may be labor-related.


--- 2c. RECOMMENDED MONITORING WORKFLOW ---

ROUTINE (weekly, ~5 minutes):
  1. Scan ALPA, AFA-CWA, SWAPA news pages for any new announcements
  2. Check NMB website for new case activity
  3. If nothing new, no action needed

DURING ACTIVE NEGOTIATIONS (daily):
  1. Monitor the relevant union's press page daily
  2. Watch for: authorization vote results, NMB release notices,
     announced strike dates, tentative agreements
  3. Update the CSV immediately when any of these occur

WHEN A NEW EVENT IS IDENTIFIED:
  1. Add a row to us_aviation_labor_actions.csv with all known fields
  2. Run: python src/fetch_prune/collect_strike_cache.py
     (validates and writes updated parquet)
  3. The prediction service picks up the new parquet on next load

AFTER AN EVENT RESOLVES:
  1. Update action_end_date in the CSV (contract ratified, strike ended)
  2. Re-run the collector


--- 2d. KEY INSIGHT: THE RAILWAY LABOR ACT GIVES YOU LEAD TIME ---

US airline strikes are NOT surprise events. The Railway Labor Act requires:
  1. Union files Section 6 notice to open negotiations
  2. Direct negotiations (months to years)
  3. NMB mediation (months)
  4. If mediation fails: NMB releases parties + 30-day cooling-off
  5. After 30 days: strike becomes legal

Strike AUTHORIZATION votes happen at step 2-3 and give weeks of advance
notice. NMB release at step 4 gives 30 days. In practice, no major US
airline crew strike has occurred since 2010 (Spirit pilots), and that was a
2-day walkout. The system is designed to prevent strikes.

The most disruptive event in recent history was the 2025 government shutdown
ATC sickouts (severity 5), which was announced by the shutdown itself —
giving days to weeks of lead time.

For the prediction model, this means:
  - You will almost always know about severity 3+ events days/weeks ahead
  - days_to_strike captures the anticipation/pre-cancellation ramp
  - carrier_delay_rate_anomaly_7d catches the actual operational impact
    even if you miss labeling an event in the CSV


================================================================================
3. CSV SCHEMA REFERENCE
================================================================================

File: ../flightrightdata/data/meta/us_aviation_labor_actions.csv

Column                Type      Description
------                ----      -----------
event_id              str       Unique ID, e.g. "2025_ATC_SHUTDOWN"
airline_iata          str       IATA code (WN, AA, DL, UA, etc.) or "ALL"
worker_type           str       pilot, flight_attendant, mechanic, ground_crew,
                                atc, dispatcher
scope                 str       airline_wide, airport_specific, system_wide
affected_airports     str       Comma-separated IATA codes, or "ALL"
announced_date        date      When the action became public knowledge
cooling_off_start     date      NMB 30-day cooling-off start (nullable)
action_start_date     date      First day of work action / vote open date
action_end_date       date      Last day of action / vote close date
workers_involved      int       Approximate headcount
action_type           str       strike, sickout, work_slowdown, lockout,
                                strike_authorization_vote, informational_picketing
severity              int       1-5 (see scale in section 1)
source                str       URL citation
notes                 str       Free text context

To add a new event: append a row, run collect_strike_cache.py, done.
